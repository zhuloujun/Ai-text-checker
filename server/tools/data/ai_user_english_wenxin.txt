In the third year of Hongwu, the garrison of Yulin was newly built beside the state seat. Commander Li Gong carried a bronze flute wherever he went. When mountain raiders came to the city one autumn, he climbed the wall and played an old tune. The soldiers, hearing the familiar sound, rose as one and drove the enemies away. After that, the flute became the sign of courage, and no one in the garrison ever feared an alarm again.

There were thirty-one forts spread across the hills of Cangwu. A Ming general once divided his men fairly among all these posts, ordering them to drill in peace and block the roads in war. Once, a soldier at the outpost fired his blunderbuss at a dark shape in the woods. The next morning, they found an ancient bronze bell marked with the year Jingkang Bingwu, struck deep with a bullet mark.
===
West of Guilin, there is a cave in Hidden Mountain. During the Song Dynasty, a scholar fled into it to escape war. On a stone table, he found old scrolls of local records, their pages still glowing like gold. When he came back later, the cave mouth had been sealed by rock, and no one has ever found the inner library since.
===
A merchant from Fuqing was blown by a storm to an island of feather-clad people. They fed him pine nuts and treated him kindly. When he missed his home, they gave him a bamboo stick that kept him safe in the waves. From then on, he could walk through stormy seas as easily as walking on a country road.
===
In the back garden of Yulin state hall, farmers once dug out an official bronze seal cast in the third year of Hongwu. It shone brighter than any later seal made in the following centuries. It was lost and found again in the Kangxi era, and the local scholar Wang Sen placed it in the storehouse of the prefecture, where it remains a treasured relic.
===
One night, a scholar was reading by the Teng River. A lady in green stepped out of the water and played the se for him. Her music was so clear that even the fish in the river stopped swimming to listen. At dawn, she said goodbye and vanished. The next day, the scholar found an ancient stone tablet half buried in the bank, an old offering text to the river spirit.
===
In front of the temple in Xingye County stood an old cypress, so thick that ten men could not circle it. During a terrible drought in the Jiajing years, the magistrate ordered it to be cut for firewood. But as soon as the flame touched the trunk, blue smoke rose and turned into rain clouds, and the land was watered at once.
===
Beneath Fubo Beach lies a huge iron cauldron, left there by General Ma Yuan during his campaign in the south. No diver could ever lift it. Once a strong swimmer tried to pull its ring, but the waves rose so fiercely that he barely escaped. No one has dared to touch it ever since.
===
Between two steep peaks in Beiliu lies the Ghost Pass. In Han times, it was said that exiled people often fell ill there. During the Wanli period, a Taoist priest wrote three large characters on the cliff. That very night, people heard soft voices in the air saying, “The pass is no longer a ghost gate now.” The miasma vanished, and travelers began to pass safely.
===
Deep in Rong Mountain, there lived a white deer whose fur shone like snow. Whenever it walked out of the forest, the local people knew that a good harvest was coming. When Wang Sen once went into the hills to collect herbs, he saw it walk slowly into a cave and disappear. On the stone, the shape of the deer could still be seen, as if carved by moonlight.
